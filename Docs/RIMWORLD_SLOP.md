# My Thoughts on AI Slop Mods on the Workshop

Creating my first game mod in 23 years really opened my eyes to how much work happens with stuff like Vanilla Expanded and the Alpha* mods and the amount of play testing and bug reports that support those creators. I truly feel like non-modders don't appreciate how much work a professional-level mod takes.

## Why we get slop mods

The side effects of AI tools:

* It's faster to create things with lower effort.
* It lowers the barrier of entry so people can create things beyond their level of skill or comprehension.

These two side effects allow people to create an overwhelming amount of slop. Slop is because of people saying "good enough" and shipping something that shouldn't have seen the light of day.

Video game mods need a lot of in-game testing. People can use AI to create the mod but most of the testing has to be done manually. Vibe coding without tests is a bad time.

It exponentially increases the signal-to-noise problem in all content on all platforms. The effort involved in creating a mod used to be a barrier that might have ensured a minimum level of quality. But it didn't.

## How I have a good time with mods in 2026

Slop mods aren't going away no matter how many mean comments people leave.

* [ ] **Pay attention to your favs** Follow the modders who do good work so you alway see their new content.
* [ ] **Mod lists** Use RimSort's save/load mod list features so you don't brick your saves. Use something like git to version control your mod lists so you can discard changes and get back to a working point.
* [ ] **Auto-saves** Keep a lot of autosaves so you can go back farther. I use [RimSaves](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1713367505)
* [ ] **Maintainability** Does it have a github and an open source license? That is a sign of potential quality and it also means someone else can take it over. Most mods get abandoned when the modder switches games.
* [ ] **Translation** Does it have a translation? The extra effort in translating a mod.
* [ ] **Let it age** When you find cool stuff on "Latest", stick them in a [workshop collection of bookmarked mods to try out](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3569495245).
  * Don't count how many mods you've bookmarked. **Never count**.
  * When you do look at them, check the comments, was the mod maintained, how many subscriptions it has, how many collections it is in. Quality indicators aren't there on Day One.
  * Poor a drink out for all the folks who install Day One mods and sacrificed their saves so you can benefit from their wisdom.
