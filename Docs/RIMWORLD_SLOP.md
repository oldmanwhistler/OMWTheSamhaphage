# My Thoughts on AI Slop Mods on the Workshop

## Why we get slop mods

Slop is what you get with low effort. The side effects of AI tools:

* It's faster to create things with lower effort.
* It lowers the barrier of entry so people can create things beyond their level of skill or comprehension.
* Because of the nature of RimWorld modding it is creates code without adding testings other than "it compiles".

Slop happens when say "good enough" and ship something that shouldn't have seen the light of day.

Video game mods need a lot of in-game testing. People can use AI to create the mod but most of the testing has to be done manually. Vibe coding without tests is a bad time on any platform/language because reading/debugging code is harder than writing code.

## How I have a good time with mods in 2026

Slop mods aren't going away no matter how many mean comments people leave.

* [ ] **Let someone else do the work** There are people doing an insane amount of effort creating and testing mod collections
  * [Ferny](https://steamcommunity.com/id/misterferny/myworkshopfiles/?section=collections&appid=294100)
  * [Samuel Streamer](https://steamcommunity.com/id/MrSamuelStreamer/myworkshopfiles/?section=collections&appid=294100) - collections created for their youtube series
  * [veequeue](https://steamcommunity.com/id/veequeue1/myworkshopfiles/?section=collections&appid=294100) - collections created for their youtube series
  * [MysteriousFawx](https://steamcommunity.com/id/mysteriousfawx/myworkshopfiles/?section=collections&appid=294100) - mod reviewer
  * [Regression](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3734979613) - tweaked version of `progression`
* [ ] **Pay attention to your favs** If you like what someone does for mods then FOLLOW THEM ON STEAM. You get a special little section called "mods from authors you follow"
* [ ] **RimSort** Use RimSort for orangizing your mods, not RimPy and not in-game mod sorters.
* [ ] **Mod lists** Use RimSort's save/load mod list features so you don't brick your saves.
  * Pro-tip stick the lists inside of a version control tool if you're a real geek.
* [ ] **Auto-saves** Keep a lot of autosaves so you can go back farther.
  * I use [RimSaves](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1713367505)
* [ ] **Check maintainability** Does it have a github and an open source license? That is a sign of potential quality. It also means there is a greater chance the mod will be (Continued) when the original modder switches games.
* [ ] **Translation** Does it have a translation? If someone thought a mod was good enough to translate, that is a quality indicator.
* [ ] **Let it age** When you find cool stuff on "latest updates", stick them in a [workshop collection of bookmarked mods to try out](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3569495245).
  * Don't count how many mods you've bookmarked. **Never count**.
  * When you do look at them, check the comments, was the mod maintained, how many subscriptions it has, how many collections it is in. Quality indicators aren't there on Day One.
