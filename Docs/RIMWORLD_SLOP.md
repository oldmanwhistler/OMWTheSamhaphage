# My Thoughts on AI Slop Mods on the Workshop

Creating a mod really opened my eyes to how much work happens with stuff like Vanilla Expanded and the Alpha* mods and the amount of play testing and bug reports that support those creators.

## My AI opinion

I'm in a field where [acquiring AI literacy is a must to keep my kids fed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JgVBqcqUGE0). There's a lot of reasons to be against AI but I'm in a field where boycotting isn't an option. It's a tool that I need to understand and know when to use.

The side effects of AI tools:

* It's faster to create things with lower effort.
* It lowers the barrier of entry to people can create things beyond their level of skill or comprehension.

These two side effects are what creates an overwhelming amount of slop. Not the fact that AI tools were used, but people saying "good enough" and shipping something that shouldn't have seen the light of day. I think specifically in the space of video game modding the problem is that people can use AI to create the mod but all the testing has to be done manually. Vibe coding without red-green TDD seems **insane** to me.

But going back to AI tools and modding, it creates a signal-to-noise problem because before the effort involved in creating a mod was a barrier that could have ensured a minimum level of quality.

## Checklist for having a good time with mods in the age of AI

* [ ] **Pay attention to the stars** Follow the modders who do good work so you alway see there new content.
* [ ] **Mod lists** Use RimSort's save/load mod list features so you don't brick your saves. Use something like VS Code and git to version control your mod lists so you can discard changes and get back to a working point.
* [ ] **Auto-saves** Keep a lot of autosaves so you can go back farther. Use [RimSaves](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1713367505)
* [ ] **Maintainability** Does it have a github and an open source license? If it does that means it can be forked and taken over by the community.
* [ ] **Let it age** Don't install new mods when they are released because there will be bugs. Keep a [workshop collection to bookmark mods to try out](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3569495245).
  * Don't look at how many mods you've bookmarked. Never look.
  * When you get to them six months later you'll be able to see if the mod was maintained or not, how many subscribes it has, how many collections it is in. All the indicators of quality to can't see the day it is first released.
